Grade

A colorist that reads
the picture first.

Generate looks at the whole clip before it touches it. Then it writes a grade for that picture and no other, checks it, and hands you every slider.

Generate

What happens when
you press it.

  1. It reads the clip

    The frame under the playhead and six more across the clip: where the blacks and whites sit, how much color there is and in which hues, how the light falls, whether there are faces.

  2. It weighs what it knows

    Every finished picture it learned from counts by how like your clip its scene is. Where they agree, the grade has it. Where they do not, it holds back.

  3. It works the grade out

    On your clip, not on a chart. The first grade is tried on three film stocks, and its sliders are set from what the result measures: tone, color, film and finish.

  4. It checks the result

    A grade that would clip a highlight, crush a black or turn the skin is eased until it does not. Then it is yours.

Again

Press again.
Get something else.

The first press gives the grade that suits the clip best. Every press after it gives a look that stands as far as it can from the ones already in the row, so you are never shown the same idea twice. The setting called Experimental decides how far a look may go.

Looks Blick 3.7

A library of 3,773 looks,
one click from your clip.

Looks is a tab of its own beside Adjust. Every look is a finished frame, shown as a picture. Press one and Blick grades the clip to it, worked out on your picture. Press two times and you see the looks close to it.

In the order that suits the clip
Relevant puts the looks that fit the frame in front of you first. Latest and Most used are the other two orders.
Six kinds
All, Dark, Bright, Warm, Cool, and Saved: the grades you have kept yourself, in the same place.
As large as you like
A Size slider goes from nine looks in a row to one. The panel widens while Looks is open, and its edge can be dragged.
Pictures or gradients
See each look as its frame, or as the two colors of its shadows and its lights.

One clip, many looks

Choose a picture.
Blick grades to it.

Each small picture is a look from the library. The large one is the same clip, graded to it.

Saved looks
Save keeps the whole grade: the look, its film, every slider, switch and curve, with a small picture. Saved looks belong to the app, so they are there in every project.
Fitted, not pasted
A saved look or a look from the library is fitted to the clip the playhead is in. The same look reads right on a night street and on a snowfield.
Out of the row again
Every look in the row has a cross. Undo puts it back.

Applies to

The whole cut, one scene,
or just this clip.

One line under the look's name says what it covers. A look for all clips is still worked out on each of them. A scene can wear its own. So can a single clip, with its own sliders, and it can go back to the cut's look without losing what it had.

Scenes Blick 3.6
Select the clips that belong together and make them a scene. A scene has its own look here and its own match in Prepare, and pressing it shows its clips alone.
Skin, under every look Blick 3.5
Every look is read as it is shown, on every clip. Where skin would carry clearly more color than it came with, the look gives way on the warm hues, for that clip alone.
The two ends Blick 3.5
On another clip a look may not send more of the picture to black or to white than on its own. Shadows or Highlights move for that clip, unseen on the sliders.

Adjust

Every number is yours.

A generated look is not a black box. It is a set of sliders and curves in five windows, and you can move any of them. Each window can be enlarged, and each can be switched off to see what it does.

Tone
Exposure, Contrast, Shadows, Highlights, Blacks and Whites, with its curves. A look alone never clips what did not clip in the frame as it arrived; your hand may.
Color
Color, Warmth and Tint, Relight, Palette, Vibrance and Crossover, and the color of the shade and of the light on their own. Skin hold says how much of their own color skin tones keep under a look.
Hues
Skies, Foliage and Reds: each turned, and given more or less color, on its own. Most skin is left where it is.
Film
A film stock as three dyes whose peaks you can move, with Density, a print's own curve and developing pulled or pushed. Then the lab: Silver, Flare, Cross process, Dye transfer, Two-color, Age, and Night for day for night.
Finish
Glow, Halation, Mist, Soften, Detail and Separation: what a lens and a print add once the color is done.
All the way Blick 3.6
The sliders go to extremes: nearly white, nearly black, one grey, four times the color. What Generate sets stays well inside; the far ends are for you.

Grain and Vignette

Off until you ask.

Every new look comes without grain and without a vignette. Both have a window of their own for the day you want them.

Grain
Amount, size, roughness, softness and color; new on every frame, slower, or still; its own weight in shadows, midtones and highlights; the film's gauge. A loupe shows a piece of the picture at 100, 200 or 400 percent, where grain can be judged.
Vignette
Amount both ways, reach, feather, falloff, roundness, stretch and angle, its weight in highlights and shadows, color, tint and hue. Its middle is placed on a small picture of the frame, and a handle on its edge sets reach and angle.

Scopes

See what the grade did.

Seven scopes at the foot of the window, as many at once as you like, in Grade and in Prepare. Blick Scope is the app's own: color and light in one drawing, in the picture's own colors.

Blick Scope
The crown says which colors and how strong. The plume says where they sit, dark at the foot and light at the top. The spine is the color of the greys: straight up is neutral. Beside them, the tones across the frame, or through time as the clip plays.
The classics
Waveform, Parade, Vector, Histogram and CIE 1931. And Numbers: what Blick Scope measures as a table, with what is clipped and what is crushed.
Look closer, and push
Pinch, or hold Cmd and scroll, to zoom into any scope where the pointer is. In Blick Scope you can take hold of the color itself: drag the crown for Warmth and Tint, the plume for Warmth and Exposure.
