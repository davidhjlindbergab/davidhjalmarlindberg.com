# davidhjalmarlindberg.com

The website of David Hjalmar Lindberg: the front page, a page for each project, and product pages for Andrum (`/andrum/`) and Blick (`/blick/`) and Blick (`/blick/`, unlisted and not on sale yet).

Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no build step. Served by GitHub Pages from the root of `main`; `CNAME` holds the custom domain.

## Pages

- **Front page** (`index.html`): it opens on "I build worlds." set exactly as wide as the page, with "Film, art, music, software" and one sentence under it. Then eleven projects in the order Nakãya, Andrum, Blick, Colobri, Avicii, The Last Testament, DINOS, Norrsken, Aware App, Talks, Music. Each links to its page.
- **Project pages**: `nakaya/`, `avicii/`, `the-last-testament/`, `dinos/`, `norrsken/`, `aware/`, `talks/` and `dj/`, each an `index.html`. Andrum, Blick and Colobri link to their product pages.
- **Plugins** (`plugins/index.html`): Andrum, Blick and Colobri, each linking to its product page. Every page's menu reads Selected work, Plugins, About, Contact; phones show the first two.
- **One stylesheet** (`style.css`) and one script (`main.js`) for the front page, the project pages, the Plugins page, Privacy and `404.html`: a nearly black ground `#0c0c0b` with ink `#f5f5f7`, and apple.com's typography since 14 Nov 2026 (SF Pro on Apple devices, Inter elsewhere, Apple's sizes, weights 400 and 600, line heights and letter spacing; a 52px bar of blurred glass that stays at the top). David asked for it again after the switch of 1 Nov was undone the same day; the product pages keep their own looks. Image corners come from `--radius` (tiles) and `--radius-lg` (full-width images), both 16px on phones. `DESIGN.md` describes the design language: colours, type sizes, components, motion and what not to do.
- **The dark** (13 Nov 2026): every page is dark. David first had words on paper and pictures in the dark, then asked to "remove the white completely and only have the dark gradients". The ground is `#0c0c0b`, the text `#f4f3ef`; on the ground lie two very large, faint fields of light (`body::before`) that drift slowly and whose colours (`--glow-a`, `--glow-b`) change over two to three minutes, under a fine grain (`body::after`). Only print is on white. `DESIGN.md` has the values.
  - The page grows a little darker as you scroll down from the header: the glow fades to 55% over the first screen (`deepen` in `style.css`, a scroll-driven animation; other browsers keep the glow as it is).
  - The glow keeps time with the clock. Each page's `<head>` has a one-line script, right after the stylesheet, that sets `--t` to the seconds since midnight (UTC); the animations begin that far in (`animation-delay`). So the light is the same on every page and does not start over between pages. A page without the line simply starts the glow from its beginning.
- **From page to page**: the pages dissolve into each other, and a project's picture travels from the front page to the top of its own page and back (`@view-transition` in `style.css`; `main.js` names the picture that was pressed). Browsers without view transitions just load the page.
- **A page's opening**: its headline is pulled into focus once, like a shot. What moves on the site is light and focus, never position; with Reduce motion on, nothing moves and only the glow's colours change.
- **Lines as wide as the page**: the front page's headline (class `fit`) and every page's e-mail address are set exactly as wide as the page: their size is the page's width divided by the width of their letters (5.19em and 12.19em in Helvetica Neue, in `style.css`). `main.js` measures the words in the visitor's own font, and as they now read, and passes the measure to the stylesheet (`--fit`, `--fit-left`), so the line also fits in Arial or after the headline has been edited. Keep the headline to one short line. Copy address, beside the address, copies it.
- **Buttons** follow apple.com: 44px pills with 17px regular text, 17px apart. On the main site the links under a project's text are buttons (`.links`: the first filled, the rest outlined); the Andrum page's `.btn` and `.play` use the same size and text. On every pill (buttons and the Colobri page's chips) the words start and end exactly where the round ends meet the straight edge: the side padding is half the height, border included (a circle at an end, like the play dot, sits centred in that round end instead). Pills are as wide as their words, never stretched by the layout (no full-width or minimum-width buttons), so this holds everywhere. The site editor keeps it when it changes a pill's height.
- **Images on hover**: on computers, a project's image on the front page and the Plugins page rests a touch darker and softer in colour. On hover the light comes first (0.6s) and the picture follows: it grows 3% over one second, starting gently and slowing to a stop (the `.project .image-frame img` rules in `style.css`). `will-change` keeps each picture on its own layer on the graphics card, so nothing is redrawn when the growth begins or ends. Phones and tablets always show the images in full. Measured on David's MacBook Pro on 14 Nov 2026: the hover runs at a steady 120 frames a second, and the images are light (the largest on the front page is 339 KB), so do not shrink them to make it smoother.
- **The Plugins menu**: hovering Plugins rolls Andrum and Colobri down under it (`.menu` in every page's header). Touch screens go straight to the Plugins page.
- **Statistics** (`stats.js`, loaded by every page, and `stats-collector.gs`): the site counts itself, without cookies or anything that identifies a visitor, so there is no cookie question for it.
  - `stats.js` sends each count to a Google Apps Script web app (its `/exec` address is `COLLECTOR` at the top). It counts page views (missing pages as 404); visits, a page view that came from outside the site, with the source (the referring site or `utm_source`), `utm_campaign`, time zone, device, browser, system and language; arrivals at Andrum or Colobri from the rest of the site; the time a page is in view; and clicks on downloads, Buy (also Andrum's in-app Buy, which opens `/andrum/buy/`), Tell me when it's on sale, e-mail links and links out.
  - `stats-collector.gs` is the script's source. It is attached to the Google Sheet "Statistik för davidhjalmarlindberg.com" in David's Google Drive and writes daily totals to its first tab, Data (day, part of the site, measure, key, count, seconds). Once a day it adds equal rows together and turns days older than 120 days into months. To change it, paste the file into the sheet's Extensions > Apps Script, save, and deploy a new version of the same web app (Deploy > Manage deployments > Edit > Version: New version), so the address in `stats.js` stays.
  - The dashboard is a Claude artifact, made from the Dashboard type, that reads the sheet through David's Google Drive connector.
  - `?stats=off` on any page stops the counting in that browser, `?stats=on` starts it again, and `?stats=test` sends that page's counts to the sheet's Test tab.
  - New pages need `<script src="/stats.js" defer></script>` in their head (and, if they use `style.css`, `<script src="/main.js" defer></script>` and the one-line clock script for the glow, copied from any other page).
- **An e-mail address before a trial download** (`download-gate.js`, 17 Nov 2026): David asked for the address of everyone who downloads a trial. Andrum's Download for Mac, Download for Windows and, on a phone, Send the link to my computer (links marked `data-gate="Andrum"`) first open a small dialog: the address, the button with the link's own words, and a line saying the address is for following up on the trial and for news now and then, linking to Privacy. (The first hour it had an unticked box for news; David took it out the same day: he wants to be able to send news to everyone.) The download starts the moment the address is in. On a phone the link to the computer then goes to the visitor's own address (`data-gate-mailto`). `/lika/download/` shows the same form in the page itself (`DownloadGate.run` with `mount`) before it forwards to the app; the Lika product page's Download the free trial goes there.
  - The address goes to the stats collector's web app (the same `/exec` address as `stats.js`), and `stats-collector.gs` writes one row per download to the tab **E-post** in the sheet "Statistik för davidhjalmarlindberg.com": Tid, E-post, Produkt, Fil, Källa, Kampanj, Sida. Rows are never added up or removed. `?stats=test` sends them to "E-post test". A hidden field catches robots, whose addresses are not sent.
  - **Buyers too** (21 Nov 2026; David: "I want to get the email to everyone that downloads or buys Andrum or Blick, even trials"): back from Polar's checkout the address carries a one-time session for the buyer. Andrum's `store.js` and Blick's `thanks/` hand it to `DownloadGate.buyer`, which asks Polar for the buyer's own name and address and files them as a purchase (the tab E-post: Typ is `köp`, a download's `nedladdning`). The tab **Adresser** is the list to write to: one row per address with name, products, Köpt, first and latest time, and a column Avregistrerad that David fills in by hand. A buyer who closes the window before the page loads is missed: Polar's own customer list is always complete.
  - The form went live (17 Nov 2026) before the collector in the sheet was updated, so each address is sent as a message the old collector also files: a download (`nedladdning`) in its tab **Test**, with the address as key ("(news)" after it from the first hour, when there was a box; Del: andrum for Andrum, sajten for Lika). Once the new `stats-collector.gs` is deployed, the first address moves those rows from Test to E-post, once (`moveEarlyLeads_`), and from then on every row lands in E-post whole.
  - The browser remembers the address (localStorage `davidhjalmarlindberg.email`), so a visitor is asked once; later downloads go straight through and are written down with the same address. Buyers are never asked: `?bought=` in the address (Lika's thank-you page links to `/lika/download/?bought=1`), Andrum's thank-you panel showing, or a purchase remembered by Andrum's `store.js`.
  - It asks, it doesn't lock: the files stay where they are, a direct link to one still works, and a browser without `<dialog>` downloads as before. The dialog holds the click back before the page's statistics, pixel and share menu see it; they count the click that follows once the address is in.
  - The look comes from `--dg-*` variables (Andrum's in `product.css`, Blick's in its store pages), with neutral dark defaults. The privacy pages (`privacy/#downloads`, Andrum's and Blick's) say what is kept, why and for how long (at most two years after the last download).
  - Blick's `terms/`, `privacy/` and `thanks/` are made by `brand/site/3.4.2/` in the Blick source (`terms.py`, `pages.py`, in the design of `blick/buy/`): change them there, or a rebuild of those pages undoes the change.

## Andrum: the page

- **The page** (`andrum/index.html`, with `andrum/assets/css/product.css` and `andrum/assets/js/product.js`; new on 14 Nov 2026): plain HTML with no build step. Black, white type and the light of Andrum's own picture. The type is Inter, as before (`andrum/assets/fonts/`): Inter Display Light for headings and figures, Inter Regular and Medium for the rest. The licence, terms and privacy pages keep `andrum/assets/css/andrum.css`.
- **The demo at the top**: a song of David's, 37 seconds, going round, three ways: Untouched, Turned down (the whole track 12.4 dB down) and Andrum. It comes from his Live set "andrumtest" (14 Nov 2026): the Music track after its EQ and limiter, and the Vocal track, exported one by one at 48 kHz with Andrum off and with Andrum on at his settings (Clarity 100 %, Max cut 21.8 dB, Start −32.9 dB, Range 15 dB, Softness 73 %, Curve Late 11, Attack 1.8 ms, Release 37.8 ms, Smooth 99 %, Lookahead 4 ms, limits 490 Hz and 1.67 kHz with 3-octave fades, Cut width 0.09 oct, Stereo linked). The page plays the two tracks summed as they are in his mixer, without the limiter on his Main track, so nothing but the music's level differs between the three; the Andrum one is the plug-in's own output from Live. The song fades in and the voice ends alone, so it is silent where it goes round. The first Listen plays the three in turn: untouched for the first sung line, turned down for the second, then Andrum; once it has been started, a small player follows down the page until it is closed. The three recordings (`andrum/assets/audio/listen-*.mp3`, 38.4 s each with half a second of lead either side) share one gain and have no limiter, and the picture is drawn from Andrum's own measurements of the song (`andrum/assets/data/demo-data.js`, which also says how long the song is, when the first Listen moves on and where the picture starts).
- **The figures beside it** (25 %, 87 %, 87 %, 12.4 dB, 4.6 dB) are measured on that song with Andrum 1.9.0's engine run offline on the same two tracks with the same settings (it matches Live's export to 43 dB under the music): clarity by Andrum's own meter while the voice sings over the music (from where it comes in to where the music starts to fade), the music's level as loudness (LUFS) over the same stretch. A new song, new settings or a new engine changes them: they are in `demo-data.js` (the readouts and the notes read them there, and so does the picture's range) and written out in the section "The same clarity, for 4.6 dB instead of 12.4."
- **Buttons**: the site's pills at 58px, the height David set on this page in the site editor (the old page carried it in a `site-editor` block; it is now in `product.css`). The small ones are 44px.
- **Store, pixel and statistics** are the same files and hooks as before: `andrum/assets/js/store.js` (`data-an-buy`, `data-an-price`, `data-an-thanks`, `data-an-key`, `#buy`; Polar's checkout returns to `/andrum/?bought=…#buy`), `andrum/assets/js/pixel.js` (`data-an-cookies`, the downloads, `data-an-send-link`) and `/stats.js`. The licence, terms and privacy pages link to `/andrum/#download`.

## The site editor (`/edit/`)

- **What it is** (`edit/index.html` and `edit/editor.js`, no build step): David's visual editor at davidhjalmarlindberg.com/edit/. He clicks text, pictures, links, fonts, sizes, colors and spacing in a live preview of a page and presses Publish, which commits straight to `main` (the messages start with `Site editor:`). It uses his own fine-grained GitHub key (this repository only, Contents: Read and write), kept in his browser; there is no server. The page is `noindex`, and its address settings (`?api=`, `?site=` and the rest) work only on localhost, for testing.
- **What it writes**: texts and attributes change in place in the page's HTML, and everything else in the file stays byte for byte. Style changes go into blocks the editor owns:
  - for one page, `<link id="site-editor-fonts">` and `<style id="site-editor">` at the end of its `<head>`;
  - for every page that uses `style.css`, an `@import … /* site-editor:fonts */` line at the top of `style.css` and a `/* site-editor:start */ … /* site-editor:end */` block at its end.
  - An element styled on its own carries a `data-se="…"` marker that its rule points to. New pictures are made web-ready (WebP, at most 2560 px) and saved next to the picture they replace, or in the page's `images/` folder.
- **Editing the site anywhere else**: pull `main` first, since pages can change from the editor at any time. Keep the editor's blocks and `data-se` markers; to tidy one up, move its rule into the page's own styles and remove the rule and its marker together. Leave `edit/` alone unless David asks.
