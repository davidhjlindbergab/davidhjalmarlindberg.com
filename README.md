# davidhjalmarlindberg.com

The website of David Hjalmar Lindberg: the front page, a page for each project, and product pages for Andrum (`/andrum/`), Colobri (`/colobri/`) and Lika (`/lika/`, not listed yet).

Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no build step. Served by GitHub Pages from the root of `main`; `CNAME` holds the custom domain.

## Pages

- **Front page** (`index.html`): it opens on "I build worlds." set exactly as wide as the page, with "Film, art, music, software" and one sentence under it. Then ten projects in the order Nakãya, Andrum, Colobri, Avicii, The Last Testament, DINOS, Norrsken, Aware App, Talks, Music. Each links to its page.
- **Project pages**: `nakaya/`, `avicii/`, `the-last-testament/`, `dinos/`, `norrsken/`, `aware/`, `talks/` and `dj/`, each an `index.html`. Andrum and Colobri link to their product pages.
- **Plugins** (`plugins/index.html`): Andrum and Colobri, each linking to its product page. Every page's menu reads Selected work, Plugins, About, Contact; phones show the first two.
- **One stylesheet** (`style.css`) and one script (`main.js`) for the front page, the project pages, the Plugins page, Privacy and `404.html`: a nearly black ground `#0c0c0b` with ink `#f4f3ef`, Helvetica Neue. Image corners come from `--radius` (tiles) and `--radius-lg` (full-width images), both 16px on phones. `DESIGN.md` describes the design language: colours, type sizes, components, motion and what not to do.
- **The dark** (13 Nov 2026): every page is dark. David first had words on paper and pictures in the dark, then asked to "remove the white completely and only have the dark gradients". The ground is `#0c0c0b`, the text `#f4f3ef`; on the ground lie two very large, faint fields of light (`body::before`) that drift slowly and whose colours (`--glow-a`, `--glow-b`) change over two to three minutes, under a fine grain (`body::after`). Only print is on white. `DESIGN.md` has the values.
  - The page grows a little darker as you scroll down from the header: the glow fades to 55% over the first screen (`deepen` in `style.css`, a scroll-driven animation; other browsers keep the glow as it is).
  - The glow keeps time with the clock. Each page's `<head>` has a one-line script, right after the stylesheet, that sets `--t` to the seconds since midnight (UTC); the animations begin that far in (`animation-delay`). So the light is the same on every page and does not start over between pages. A page without the line simply starts the glow from its beginning.
- **From page to page**: the pages dissolve into each other, and a project's picture travels from the front page to the top of its own page and back (`@view-transition` in `style.css`; `main.js` names the picture that was pressed). Browsers without view transitions just load the page.
- **A page's opening**: its headline is pulled into focus once, like a shot. What moves on the site is light and focus, never position; with Reduce motion on, nothing moves and only the glow's colours change.
- **Lines as wide as the page**: the front page's headline (class `fit`) and every page's e-mail address are set exactly as wide as the page: their size is the page's width divided by the width of their letters (5.19em and 12.19em in Helvetica Neue, in `style.css`). `main.js` measures the words in the visitor's own font, and as they now read, and passes the measure to the stylesheet (`--fit`, `--fit-left`), so the line also fits in Arial or after the headline has been edited. Keep the headline to one short line. Copy address, beside the address, copies it.
- **Buttons** follow apple.com: 44px pills with 17px regular text, 17px apart. On the main site the links under a project's text are buttons (`.links`: the first filled, the rest outlined); the Andrum page's `.btn` and `.play` use the same size and text. On every pill (buttons and the Colobri page's chips) the words start and end exactly where the round ends meet the straight edge: the side padding is half the height, border included (a circle at an end, like the play dot, sits centred in that round end instead). Pills are as wide as their words, never stretched by the layout (no full-width or minimum-width buttons), so this holds everywhere. The site editor keeps it when it changes a pill's height.
- **Images on hover**: on computers, a project's image on the front page and the Plugins page rests a touch darker and softer in colour. On hover the light comes first (0.6s) and the picture follows: it grows 3% over one second, starting gently and slowing to a stop (the `.project .image-frame img` rules in `style.css`). `will-change` keeps each picture on its own layer on the graphics card, so nothing is redrawn when the growth begins or ends. Phones and tablets always show the images in full. Measured on David's MacBook Pro on 14 Nov 2026: the hover runs at a steady 120 frames a second, and the images are light (the largest on the front page is 339 KB), so do not shrink them to make it smoother.
- **The Plugins menu**: hovering Plugins rolls Andrum and Colobri down under it (`.menu` in every page's header). Touch screens go straight to the Plugins page.
- **Statistics** (`stats.js`, loaded by every page, and `stats-collector.gs`): the site counts itself, without cookies or anything that identifies a visitor, so there is no cookie question for it.
  - `stats.js` sends each count to a Google Apps Script web app (its `/exec` address is `COLLECTOR` at the top). It counts page views (missing pages as 404); visits, a page view that came from outside the site, with the source (the referring site or `utm_source`), `utm_campaign`, time zone, device, browser, system and language; arrivals at Andrum or Colobri from the rest of the site; the time a page is in view; and clicks on downloads, Buy (also Andrum's in-app Buy, which opens `/andrum/buy/`), Tell me when it's on sale, e-mail links and links out.
  - `stats-collector.gs` is the script's source. It is attached to the Google Sheet "Statistik för davidhjalmarlindberg.com" in David's Google Drive and writes daily totals to its first tab, Data (day, part of the site, measure, key, count, seconds). Once a day it adds equal rows together and turns days older than 120 days into months. To change it, paste the file into the sheet's Extensions > Apps Script, save, and deploy a new version of the same web app (Deploy > Manage deployments > Edit > Version: New version), so the address in `stats.js` stays.
  - The dashboard is a Claude artifact, made from the Dashboard type, that reads the sheet through David's Google Drive connector.
  - `?stats=off` on any page stops the counting in that browser, `?stats=on` starts it again, and `?stats=test` sends that page's counts to the sheet's Test tab.
  - New pages need `<script src="/stats.js" defer></script>` in their head (and, if they use `style.css`, `<script src="/main.js" defer></script>` and the one-line clock script for the glow, copied from any other page).
- **Meta pixel on Andrum** (`andrum/assets/js/pixel.js`): the same pixel as Colobri (4475247569405091, the dataset named davidhjalmarlindberg.com in Events Manager), asked for with the same cookie question and the same saved answer (`colobri.cookies`), so a visitor answers once for the whole site. It sends PageView, ViewContent, StartTrial (Mac and Windows downloads) and Lead (Tell me when it's on sale). Cookie settings is in the page's footer.
- **Privacy** (`privacy/index.html`): what the site counts, the Meta pixel and cookies, hosting. The footers link to it.
- **Colobri's type**: SF Pro through the system font, with Inter (`fonts/inter-var-latin.woff`, SIL Open Font Licence in `fonts/OFL.txt`) on other devices.
- **Images**: front-page images in `img/work/`, each project's gallery in `img/<project>/`.
- **The Music page** (`dj/`, named Music since David renamed its tile on the front page) plays the Borderland 2026 set through SoundCloud's own player, in its artwork view (`visual=true`), which suits the dark page.
- **Voice**: the site speaks in the first person on every page. `DESIGN.md`, part 11, has the rules for its texts.

## Lika: the page

- **The page** (`lika/index.html`): plain HTML with its styles and its one script inside, no build step, like Colobri's. Lika is a Mac app that matches a set of AI-generated clips to each other and finishes them. The ground is the logo's own blue-black `#05080b`, the text `#f4f3ef`, and the logo's light is the only colour (`--light`, orange through pink and violet to blue, and `--light-2`, the same turned round): in the mark, in the small equals sign before each label and in what a diagram points at. The type is Colobri's (the system font, Inter elsewhere). It loads `/stats.js` and nothing else from outside: no Meta pixel, no cookies.
- **Not listed yet** (14 Nov 2026): the page is live at `/lika/` but has `<meta name="robots" content="noindex">` and is not in the menu, on the Plugins page, on the front page or in `sitemap.xml`. To announce it: remove the robots line, add Lika to the Plugins menu in every page's header and to `plugins/index.html` (copy its facts from this page), and add it to the sitemap.
- **Price**: €129, one licence, VAT included. The terms under it (one person, two Macs, every 1.x update, any work, 14 days) are Andrum's, to be confirmed. There is no checkout yet: every button is "Tell me when it's on sale", an e-mail link whose subject has "sale" in it, which `stats.js` counts as `notify`.
- **The pictures** (`lika/images/`): none of them is footage. They are six test shots drawn in code (three white spheres on pale sand in low sun, each shot given its own drift in level and colour) and what Lika made of them. `lika-film.mp4` is the cut as the shots arrived and then the same cut matched, in one file; the page's Original and Final switch jumps to the same moment in the other half. `app-original-*.webp` and `app-final-*.webp` are two photographs of the app's window with those clips, `shot-1.webp` to `shot-6.webp` the strip, `grain-*.webp` one detail with each kind of grain. All of it is made in the Lika build (`brand/demo/shots.py`, `capture.js`, `assets.py`). Replace them with real footage when there is some David wants to show.
- **The numbers** in the table (how far apart the shots sat) are read from the app's own panel in that run. Change them if the pictures are made again.
- **The logo** (`lika-mark.svg`, `lika-logo.svg`, `lika-mark-small.svg`, `lika-logo-small.svg`): an equals sign of two frames, each with a window, the upper lit from its left and the lower from its right, each with one corner dying off into the dark. Drawn after David's picture of 14 Nov 2026 by `brand/logo.py` in the Lika build. The page's large mark (`lika-mark-320.webp`, `lika-mark-640.webp`) has the grain on it; under about 40 px the mark is two plain bars (`lika-mark-small.svg`, the favicon, and `lika-logo-small.svg` in the header and footer).
- **Motion**: what moves is light and focus. The headline and each section come into focus once as they are reached (opacity and blur, never position), the light behind the mark breathes, the film plays only while it is in view, and the window turns from Original to Final by itself the first time it is seen. With Reduce motion on, nothing moves: the film waits with its controls and the window shows Final.

## The site editor (`/edit/`)

- **What it is** (`edit/index.html` and `edit/editor.js`, no build step): David's visual editor at davidhjalmarlindberg.com/edit/. He clicks text, pictures, links, fonts, sizes, colors and spacing in a live preview of a page and presses Publish, which commits straight to `main` (the messages start with `Site editor:`). It uses his own fine-grained GitHub key (this repository only, Contents: Read and write), kept in his browser; there is no server. The page is `noindex`, and its address settings (`?api=`, `?site=` and the rest) work only on localhost, for testing.
- **What it writes**: texts and attributes change in place in the page's HTML, and everything else in the file stays byte for byte. Style changes go into blocks the editor owns:
  - for one page, `<link id="site-editor-fonts">` and `<style id="site-editor">` at the end of its `<head>`;
  - for every page that uses `style.css`, an `@import … /* site-editor:fonts */` line at the top of `style.css` and a `/* site-editor:start */ … /* site-editor:end */` block at its end.
  - An element styled on its own carries a `data-se="…"` marker that its rule points to. New pictures are made web-ready (WebP, at most 2560 px) and saved next to the picture they replace, or in the page's `images/` folder.
- **Editing the site anywhere else**: pull `main` first, since pages can change from the editor at any time. Keep the editor's blocks and `data-se` markers; to tidy one up, move its rule into the page's own styles and remove the rule and its marker together. Leave `edit/` alone unless David asks.
